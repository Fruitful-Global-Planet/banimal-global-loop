## 2. Market Context (verified facts)

- **Regions:** Zimbabwean arabica grows only in the Eastern Highlands (Manicaland): Chipinge (≈75% of specialty output), Chimanimani, Mutasa, Mutare incl. Vumba Mountains, and Honde Valley (≈850 masl). Altitudes ~1,000–1,500 masl. Dominant varieties: Catimor (90%+ in Chipinge), SL-14/28/34, Caturra. Harvest May–September. (Sources: omwani.com sourcing guide; perfectdailygrind.com; sweetmarias.com.)
- **Volume reality:** National production collapsed from ~15,000 t/yr (1980s peak) to ~400–500 t/yr. In 2024 Zimbabwe exported 193 t; 2025 ~158 t but earned more due to global price rally (instagram.com/reel/DafB5GJs6yf). ~300–800 active smallholder farmers; recovery driven by Nespresso's Reviving Origins + TechnoServe (2017–) which pays farmers in USD and launched single-origin "Tamuka Mu Zimbabwe" in 18 countries in 2019. (nestle.com; technoserve.org; nationalgeographic.com.)
- **Green coffee price benchmarks:** ZW AA-grade green averaged ≈ **USD 6/kg** (H1-2025: 195,000 kg / USD 902,311 ≈ USD 4.63/kg blended; AA higher) (Facebook: Zimbabwe coffee growers group); reported ZW export price ≈ USD 3.89/kg (selinawamucii.com, May 2026); import reference CIF USD 6.6/kg (Creation Hotel Amenities / Caturra Africa distributor FB). Global roasted wholesale reference USD 11–12.63/kg (tridge.com).
- **Roasted retail benchmarks (Zim origin, international roasters):** USD 14.99–22.99 per 12 oz (~USD 44–67/kg) (Red Rooster, Out of the Grey); ZiMM Switzerland CHF 19.50/200 g sub (~CHF 97/kg); Changamiri (UK) £8.75–£21.00/bag. Local ZW roaster prices are lower (see table).
- **Heritage/consolidation note:** the old export channel (Zimbabwe Coffee Millers, Mutare) is defunct (businessdaily.co.zw, 2013); today's supply chains are small estates + young roasters + NGO-supported smallholders. Buying *roasted* beans from a domestic roaster avoids all export licensing; buying *farm-gate green* to self-roast triggers milling/export paperwork only if exported — within Zimbabwe it is a domestic trade (see §7).

## 6. Kiosk Economics (COGS check for 5 kg/week)

- Assumption: 12 oz cup = double shot ≈ **18 g** roasted coffee → 1 kg ≈ 55 cups; 5 kg/wk ≈ 275 cups/wk ≈ 40/day (realistic kiosk pace).
- Coffee bean cost at **USD 22–28/kg** (VFCC-range local wholesale): **USD 0.40–0.50/cup**.
- Milk (~150 ml @ ~USD 1.2/L) + cup/lid/stirrer + sugar ≈ USD 0.30–0.45/cup.
- **All-in COGS ≈ USD 0.70–0.95/cup → 12–19% of a USD 5 cup (well under the 30% cap).** Even at USD 40/kg imported-retail pricing, coffee-only = USD 0.72/cup and all-in stays ≈ 22–24%.
- Monthly bean spend: 22.5 kg × USD 22–28 ≈ **USD 500–630/month** — fits a kiosk budget. Negotiate a standing monthly price-lock + volume discount (20–25 kg/mo is a meaningful order for a micro-roaster).

## 7. Logistics & Regulatory Notes (Victoria Falls delivery)

1. **Primary route (recommended): local VF roasters.** VFCC and Loretta's both roast in Victoria Falls from Eastern Highlands beans; beans move *inside* Zimbabwe (green beans couriered by bus from Chipinge/Chimanimani/Vumba → VF), then are roasted locally. You buy roasted beans in town: no border, no export licence, USD or ZAR cash/EcoCash-style local payment, freshest roast, zero freight risk. Weekly standing order or same-day local delivery.
2. **Harare partner/consolidation route (backup):** House of Legends (Harare) or Coffee Snobs (phone orders) → consolidated monthly pallet/parcel via road freight (Harare–Bulawayo–Victoria Falls ≈ 1,500 km; 1–3 days via freighters like Swift/Zimpost/Greyhound-style parcel services; ~USD 30–60 for 25 kg depending on carrier — confirm quotes). Roast-date freshness: order roast-to-ship so beans arrive within ~7–10 days of roasting.
3. **Farm-gate green buying (only if self-roasting later):** buying green from estates (Fountain Creek, Zimunda, Pezuru-type) within Zimbabwe is domestic trade — no export licence needed; but you then need: a roaster (capex USD 2–6k for a 1–2 kg drum roaster), shelf-life management (green stores 6–12 months; roasted 2–4 weeks), and a municipal food/premises licence for processing. For kiosk scale, this is phase-2; start with roasted beans.
4. **If ever exporting/buying cross-border (e.g., from Zambia side of the Falls):** green/roasted movement across the Zambezi bridge requires ZIMRA export declarations + (for green) ZW Coffee Industry export channel/milling certification; VAT/withholding on cross-border services applies. Keep purchases domestic to sidestep all of this.
5. **Payment:** local roasters accept USD (widely used in VF) and increasingly ZWL/RTGS via bank transfer or mobile money; Harare roasters invoice in USD or ZWL by arrangement. Confirm wholesale invoice (needed for your books) at first order.
