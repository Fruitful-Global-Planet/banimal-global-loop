## 3. The Banimal Giving Loop model

- **Every item sold = 1 fresh pannekoek given to a child in need, TODAY** (daily drop)
- **Giving units per order:** coffee +1 · smoothie +1 · single pancake +1 · 6-piece box +6 · 12-pack +12
- **Example till moment:** *"2 Coffee with Chocolate + a 6-piece Milk Pannekoek"* = R155, **8 items = 8 children fed today**
- **Giveaway cost:** R0.75 actual per pancake · accounted at R1.90 · promoted at R5.00 value (6.7× marketing leverage)
- **Baseline cost:** 2,850 items/mo × R0.75 = **R2,138/mo = 1.9% of turnover** (at R1.90 accounting: R5,415 = 4.8%)

## 5. The discussion — what this does to sales

**Sales drivers:**
1. Basket builder — customers add items (6-piece box) to give more
2. Daily frequency ritual — "my coffee feeds a child today"
3. Differential vs Starbucks/McCafé in the mall — a same-day feeding loop no one can copy
4. Marketing leverage 6.7× — R0.75 cost promoted at R5.00 value
5. Franchise pitch strengthens — franchisees sell purpose

**Costs & risks:**
- Giveaway cost scales with sales (controlled %: 1.9% of turnover at R0.75)
- **Break-even: the loop pays for itself at just ~+3% more items sold** (R2,138 ÷ R24.40 blended contribution ≈ 88 units)
- Daily-drop logistics: partner route (school/feeding scheme) collecting each afternoon (e.g., 16:00 window)
- Giveaway contains egg (allergen — simple warning needed); cap giving units per order (e.g., max 12)
